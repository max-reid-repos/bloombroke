## 5. Hindsight figures and calculators are illustrations

- **WHATIF** shows what money spent in the past would be worth in a company's shares today. It uses hindsight: it picks the result after the fact, ignores costs, taxes, dividends and currency effects, and assumes a purchase nobody could have known to make. It is not a prediction and not a recommendation to buy any stock.
- **AFFORD**, **LOAN** and **COMPOUND** are simple calculators. Their results depend on the numbers you type and on fixed assumptions, such as an assumed yearly return. They are rough illustrations, not forecasts, quotes or advice. AFFORD is about things you buy, like a bike or a laptop. It does not assess investments.
- **SCREEN**, **HEATMAP**, **MOVERS** and similar lists sort data mechanically. Being on a list says nothing about whether something is a good or bad investment.

## 9. Where the data comes from

- Prices and charts: CNBC. US stocks: Nasdaq Last Sale.
- RT means real time. DLY means delayed: futures about 10 minutes, indexes about 15 minutes.
- Company data, calendars and the stock screener: Nasdaq. Filings and financial statements: US SEC EDGAR.
- Headlines: CNBC, MarketWatch, Yahoo Finance, Nasdaq, Seeking Alpha, the Federal Reserve, BLS, SEC EDGAR, GlobeNewswire, PR Newswire, Business Wire and Reddit (r/wallstreetbets). Each headline links to the original publisher.

- Rates: US Treasury, Federal Reserve Bank of New York and Freddie Mac. Economy: FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. Inflation: US Bureau of Labor Statistics.
- Economic calendar: Forex Factory. Options: Cboe, delayed 15 minutes. Crypto: CoinGecko.
